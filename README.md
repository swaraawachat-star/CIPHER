# CIPHER
# CIPHER – Interactive Mechanical Puzzle Box

**CAD Software:** Onshape  
**Project Category:** Mechanical Engineering / CAD Design  
**Development Status:** Concept Development and Mechanism Planning

---

## 1. About the Project

CIPHER is an interactive mechanical puzzle box that challenges the player to unlock a concealed compartment by completing a sequence of mechanical interactions.

The main idea behind this project is to create something that goes beyond a conventional combination lock. Instead of simply entering a code, the player has to experiment with different movements, observe how the mechanism responds and gradually figure out how the different components work together.

The puzzle will primarily use rotating and sliding mechanisms, with a final release mechanism that allows access to the hidden compartment.

I chose this project to explore mechanical design and understand how multiple moving components can be integrated into a single functional assembly.

## 2. Concept and Gameplay

The puzzle is designed around a trial-and-error approach. The player will not be directly given the correct combination or sequence.

The proposed interaction is divided into three main stages.

### Stage 1 – Rotating Disc Alignment

The first stage consists of three independently rotating discs arranged next to each other.

- Each disc will have six distinguishable positions.
- An internal notch will be incorporated into each disc.
- The player will have to experiment with the rotations to find the correct alignment.
- A common locking bar will interact with the three discs.

The locking bar should only be able to complete its movement when all three notches are aligned correctly.

### Stage 2 – Sliding Mechanism

After the first mechanism is released, the player will be able to operate a horizontal slider.

- The slider will move along a guided path.
- Its movement will expose or provide access to a secondary release lever.
- The slider should remain restricted until the first locking mechanism has been disengaged.

### Stage 3 – Secondary Release

The final stage involves a secondary lever connected to the compartment latch.

- Operating the lever will disengage the latch.
- The concealed compartment will then be able to slide open.
- The compartment should be capable of being closed and reset for another attempt.

## 3. Proposed Mechanical Components

The following components have been identified for the initial design.

**Rotating assembly**
- 3 rotating discs
- 3 individual axles
- Spacers for maintaining disc separation
- Alignment notches
- Position markings

**Locking assembly**
- Common locking bar
- Locking bar guide rails
- Mechanical stops
- Connecting elements

**Release assembly**
- Horizontal slider
- Slider guide rails
- Secondary release lever
- Compartment latch

**External structure**
- Main enclosure
- Sliding compartment
- Removable top cover
- Fasteners

The exact dimensions and geometry of these components will be finalised during CAD development.

## 4. Initial Design Considerations

Before beginning the complete CAD assembly, I identified a few important mechanical requirements that need to be considered.

**Independent disc movement:** Each disc must rotate independently without causing unwanted movement in the other discs.

**Locking bar interaction:** The geometry of the notches and locking bar needs to be designed carefully so that an incorrectly positioned disc physically prevents the bar from completing its movement.

**Component clearance:** There should be sufficient space between the discs, axles, locking bar and surrounding structure to avoid interference during movement.

**Guided sliding motion:** Both the main slider and compartment should move along defined paths rather than shifting unpredictably.

**Reset mechanism:** The puzzle should be reusable, so all the components must be capable of returning to their original locked positions.

## 5. Proposed Initial Dimensions

These are preliminary dimensions for the first CAD layout and may change after testing the mechanism.

- Main enclosure: 120 × 80 × 50 mm
- Rotating disc diameter: 28 mm
- Rotating disc thickness: 5 mm
- Disc centre-to-centre spacing: approximately 32–34 mm
- Axle diameter: approximately 4 mm
- Locking bar: approximately 65 × 6 × 3 mm

These dimensions are being treated as starting references rather than final manufacturing specifications.

## 6. CAD Development Plan

The modelling process will be divided into smaller stages to avoid designing the entire assembly before understanding how the central mechanism works.

**Phase 1 – Rotating Disc Assembly**
- Create the geometry of one rotating disc.
- Add the central axle hole and alignment notch.
- Establish the six indexed positions.
- Duplicate the component for the remaining two discs.
- Design individual axles and spacers.

**Phase 2 – Locking Mechanism**
- Develop the common locking bar.
- Design its guide structure.
- Check how the bar interacts with aligned and misaligned discs.
- Evaluate possible interference during movement.

**Phase 3 – Sliding and Release Mechanisms**
- Model the horizontal slider.
- Design the secondary lever.
- Develop the compartment latch.
- Establish the movement of the concealed compartment.

**Phase 4 – Complete Assembly**
- Assemble all the individual components in Onshape.
- Apply appropriate revolute and slider mates.
- Check component clearances.
- Test the different mechanical positions.
- Refine the design wherever necessary.

## 7. Software and Design Tools

- **Onshape:** Part modelling and assembly development.
- **Sketch tools:** Planning profiles, dimensions and component geometry.
- **Assembly mates:** Defining rotational and sliding movement.
- **Interference detection:** Identifying potential collisions between components.
- **Exploded views:** Presenting the arrangement of individual parts.

## 8. Current Development Status

The initial concept and gameplay sequence have been established. The three-stage mechanism has been broken down into its individual components, and preliminary dimensions have been identified for the rotating assembly.

The main challenge at this point is figuring out the exact geometry of the interaction between the three rotating discs and the common locking bar. This needs to be resolved before proceeding with the complete assembly.

## 9. Future Improvements

Some additional features that may be considered once the basic mechanism is functional include:

- More detailed position markings on the rotating discs.
- A more compact internal arrangement.
- Improved guidance for the sliding components.
- A transparent or removable cover to make the internal mechanism visible.
- A cleaner reset mechanism.
- An exploded assembly view and detailed technical drawings.

## 10. Project Objective

The objective of CIPHER is to develop a reusable mechanical puzzle that combines rotational and linear motion through a sequence of interconnected mechanisms.

The project will focus on understanding component relationships, designing functional mechanical constraints and resolving interference issues through iterative CAD development.

Rather than concentrating only on the appearance of the puzzle box, the priority is to make the internal mechanism work reliably and create a complete, well-organised CAD assembly.
